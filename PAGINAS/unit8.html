Answer key & explanations

These are the correct answers for the exercises in the students' printed book (pages 64–71), with a simple explanation of the rule behind each one — so you can correct their books directly. We can't reprint the book's own questions or pictures here, only the answers and the reasoning.

Lesson 1 (SB p.64) — Words, Activity 1

📝 Look at the picture and write the food word for each number.

1 pasta · 2 vegetables · 3 milk · 4 noodles · 5 water · 6 sandwiches · 7 soup · 8 salad · 9 tea · 10 chicken · 11 cheese · 12 coffee
Each number matches a food item in the picture. Look carefully at the container and colour to tell similar items apart (e.g. tea and coffee are both in a cup).

Lesson 1 (SB p.64) — Words, Activity 2

📝 Match each person to what they've got, then complete the sentence.

The man has got a bottle of water. · The girl has got a plate of pasta. · The woman has got a cup of tea. · The boy has got a glass of orange juice. · There is a bowl of soup.
Match each container word (bowl/glass/bottle/cup/plate) to its shape in the picture — a bottle is for drinks you carry, a bowl is for soup, etc.

Lesson 2 (SB p.65) — Reading & Speaking, Activity 1

📝 Put each word in the right group: drinks, meat, fruit, vegetables or other food.

Drinks: tea, coffee, milk, lemonade, water, hot chocolate, cola · Meat: chicken, a sausage · Fruit: orange, apple, lemon, watermelon, pear, grapes, strawberries, cherries, mango, lime · Vegetables: peas, potatoes, tomatoes, cucumber, beans, onions, peppers · Other food: butter, cheese, soup, sandwiches, crisps, cake, biscuits, ice cream
These are suggested answers — the book accepts any reasonable word that fits the category, this is just a starting list.

Lesson 2 (SB p.65) — Reading & Speaking, Activity 2

📝 Read the clue and complete the missing word.

1 bread → sandwich · 2 vegetable → carrot · 3 drink → coffee · 4 juice → bottle
Each gapped definition already gives the first letter of the missing word as a clue — read the whole sentence first to guess the item, then check the letter matches.

Lesson 3 (SB p.66) — Story, Activity 2

📝 Complete each sentence with a, an or some.

1 an · 2 some · 3 some · 4 a · 5 some · 6 some  |  Uncountable nouns: lemonade, chocolate
Use an before a vowel sound, a before a consonant sound, and some when the food is plural or uncountable (you can't count it as one unit — like lemonade or chocolate).

Lesson 4 (SB p.67) — Language practice, Activity 1

📝 Find the 5 differences between the two pictures and complete the sentences.

1 cakes · 2 sandwiches · 3 juice · 4 chocolate · 5 presents
Compare the two pictures item by item — these are the five things that changed between "before" and "after" the party.

Lesson 4 (SB p.67) — Language practice, Activity 3

📝 Complete the questions with how much or how many.

1 water · 2 apples · 3 cheese · 4 bananas
Use how much with uncountable nouns (water, cheese) and how many with countable nouns in the plural (apples, bananas).

Lesson 5 (SB p.68) — Language practice, Activity 3

📝 Look at the pictures and write what they have to do, using have to or has to.

1 The children have to say the alphabet. · 2 The boy has to carry a big bag. · 3 The children have to sit on the floor. · 4 The girl has to feed the fish. · 5 The children have to water the plants. · 6 The children have to go outside.
Use has to only with he/she/it (a single boy or girl); everyone else (the children) uses have to.
